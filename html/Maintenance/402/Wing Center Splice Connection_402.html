Details of the wing center-splice connection are shown below in ref

The main spar caps of each wing are attached to each other with p/n 20591-1/-2 (upper pair) and 21087-1/-2 (lower pair) attach blocks. The main spar webs are joined together by a p/n 20990-2 splice plate of .47" thick heat-treated 4130 steel. The connection to the fuselage on the aircraft centerline is made with a .063" thick 4130N steel plate (p/n 20178-1) fastened to the fuselage structure with two AN5-23A bolts and through the 20591-1 upper spar cap attach block with two NAS1312-76 bolts. There are four p/n 20177-1 tube-nut welded assemblies and two p/n 21049-1 sleeves used in each aircraft installation. After the tube-nuts have been fully tightened, the sleeves and tube-nuts are initially drilled to 9/32", reamed to .3125" and two AN5-16A bolts installed in each sleeve. If the wings are removed from the aircraft, it is usually necessary to replace the four tube-nut assemblies and two sleeves since the .3125" reamed holes in both parts will most likely not line up during reassembly. In this case, tighten new tube-nuts with 1920 inch-pounds of torque, position the new sleeves midway between the tube-nuts, drill 9/32" and then ream to .3125" through the sleeve and tube-nut in preparation for the installation of AN5-16A bolts.

The tube-nut and sleeve installation is to take the “kick-load" imposed by dihedral angle and cancels it out by transferring this load from the upper spar caps to the lower spar caps.

Torque values should be checked on the nuts to learn if the bolts have elongated or if fretting has loosened the fit of the joint. Torque values to be checked are as follows:

There are seven NAS1306-24 bolts joining the small leg of each upper spar cap to the 20990-2 splice plate on the aft side of the web. There are an additional six NAS1306-24 bolts, one NAS1306-23 bolt and two NAS1306-20 bolts through the small leg of each lower spar cap (along with six NAS1306-20 bolts through each spar web plate). These 3/8" fasteners are torqued to 300 inch-pounds at the nut. The four NAS1304-24 bolts and five NAS1304-20 bolts passing through the 20990-2 plate and spar caps have a torque of 100 inch-pounds applied to the nut. The sixteen NAS1204-24 screws have a torque of 80 in-lbs applied to the nuts.

2000 Hour Center Splice Inspection

  • Remove inspection covers and side skins to gain access to the wing center splice area.
  • With a mirror, borescope, or other appropriate tool, visually inspect the center splice bolts. Specifically, look for signs of corrosion, bolt damage or looseness, fretting or any other signs that the center splice may require more in-depth inspection.
  • Inspect the entire splice connection for cracks. The critical area for cracks is the lower spar cap at station 6.38" from the aircraft centerline where the 3/8" NAS bolt attaches the 21087-1 and -2 blocks to the main leg of the cap. If a crack were to develop at that point, it would probably progress to the small leg of the cap at station 6.53" where the most inboard of the NAS1306 bolts are located. It would also be advisable to carefully check the lower side of the main leg in the area where the fuel tank bottom ends.
  • If issues are noted that cannot be remedied (corrosion removal and touch up) through the access holes or side skins, remove the hopper for further inspection and repair.

For appropriate inspection intervals of the wing center splice connection, see of this manual. If a new wing is to be installed, contact the factory for wing installation instructions at the time the wing is ordered.

To aid in removal of the wings, the hopper should be removed.

Consult the factory for tools, jigs and fixtures to ensure proper installation of a new wing.
