The tail wheel is Cleveland p/n 40-168.

Details of the Cleveland tail wheel are shown in Fig Cross Reference.

The Air Tractor tail wheel bearings are Timken 07100 cone (the part with the rollers) and 07196 cup (the part pressed into the wheel half). Hand pack the bearings with MIL-G-81322 grease and install wheel on the axle. Install the 40051-1 tongue washer and 40134-1 jam nut on the axle with the round shoulder inboard and tighten to 120 in-lb. All the bearing slack should be removed but the wheel is free to turn. Inflate the tire to 50 psi. Grease the axle bolt and install wheel and axle assembly on the fork. Torque the axle nut to 300 inch-pounds. Safety the jam nut against the rotation of the wheel with MS20995C32 stainless safety wire.

Keeping the bearings properly lubricated is the key to long bearing life.

To remove the tail wheel, jack the wheel clear of the ground per Section . and remove the long AN6C63A bolt that attaches the axle to the fork. Note the method of safety wire on the large stainless jam nut and cut the wire loose from the fork. The tail wheel is now free and ready for disassembly. Back off the jam nut from the axle and remove the axle. Inspect the axle for wear at the bearing positions, as insufficient greasing procedures will cause the bearing to turn on the axle and ruin both parts. Inspect the AN6 bolt for wear and straightness, and slide the bolt through the fork to check the hole in the fork for wear. There should be no wear in the fork holes since there is no movement between fork and bolt. If the tire is to be removed, deflate and remove the bolts holding the wheel halves together.

Before reassembly, clean all parts. Partially inflate the tube to keep from pinching it during installation. Reassembly is opposite of disassembly.

The axle is to be installed from the left side to the right (nuts are on right side). Verify that the axle's anti rotation tab engages with the left hand arm of the fork as shown in Fig Cross Reference. If the axle or fork have been replaced, slight grinding may be required to ensure proper engagement.
