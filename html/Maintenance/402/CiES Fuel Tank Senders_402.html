Beginning with 402-1555, Air Tractor began installing fuel quantity sending elements from CiES. Each of the fuel tanks has two fuel-quantity sending elements. The outboard fuel sender is connected to the inboard fuel sender electrically, and all fuel senders require active power to function. The outboard sending element is active in sensing fuel level when the fuel level is in the upper third of the fuel tank. The inboard sending element is active when the fuel level is in the lower third of the tank. Both of the elements are active when the fuel level is in the mid-range. The inboard and outboard fuel senders are located on the inboard and outboard fuel tank wing ribs. The fuel senders are statically grounded to the wing through burnishing one of the mounting holes on the fuel sender and the AN3-6A bolt attaching the fuel sender to the rib and electrically grounded to the airplane electrical system.

The inboard fuel tank sender is p/n 63452-1 (left hand) and 63452-2 (right hand) for all MVP-50T-equipped airplanes. The inboard tank sender is p/n 63452-15 (left hand) and 63452-15 (right hand) for AT-402 models with 234 gallon fuel capacity. The outboard fuel tank sender for all models is p/n 63451-1 (left hand) and 63451-2 (right hand). Each fuel sender is attached with five AN3-6A bolts (torqued to 16 - 18 inch-pounds) and five AN960-10 washers. Each fuel sender is supplied with a gasket to seal against the wing rib, and the use of additional fuel tank sealants is unnecessary.

Troubleshooting

When the aircraft is initially powered on, the fuel senders will output a 5V DC signal to the gauging system, then graudally reduce signal output to match the fuel level in the wing tank. This will be the easiest method to confirm the functionality of the fuel senders.

There are separate methods for verifying the functionality of the inboard and outboard fuel senders. Both fuel senders must be functioning properly for the fuel quantity instrument to show any fuel level information.

To verify functionality of the outboard fuel senders:

  1. Remove the skin at the wing root to expose the fuel sender interconnect cable assembly.
  2. Ensure the battery is turned on and supplying power to the fuel
  3. Using a multimeter set to DC Volts, measure the voltage output on the "freq. out" wire from the outboard fuel sender. The reading should be between 2.4 and 2.7 volts for a properly functioning outboard fuel sender.
  4. If the voltage reading is below 0.7 volts or above 3.0 volts, the outboard fuel sender is likely faulty and should be replaced.

To verify functionality of the inboard fuel senders:

  1. Ensure the outboard fuel sender is functioning and connected properly.
  2. Using a multimeter set to DC volts, measure the voltage output on the "volt out" wire from the inboard fuel sender. Raising and lowering the inboard fuel sender arm should cause the voltage to vary between 0 and 5 volts.
  3. If the voltage reading does not vary as the inboard fuel sender arm is moved, verify the connection of the outboard sender. If the outboard fuel sender is connected and functioning properly, the inboard fuel sender is faulty and should be replaced.

If these procedures did not identify the cause of the issue, inspect for damaged wires, damaged insulators, or loose plug connections. Inadvertent grounding can cause issues with fuel sender functionality.

Sender Replacement

When the sender is being replaced, do not bend the fuel sender arm. Ensure the fuel sender arm can travel through the full stroke unimpeded.Install the proper replacement fuel sender per the Aircraft Parts Manual and torque the bolts to 18 in-lbs. Inboard fuel senders are both fuel tank capacity and left/right hand specific, so ensure the correct part number is installed on the correct wing.
