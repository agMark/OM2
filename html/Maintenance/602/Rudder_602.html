Details of the rudder are shown in Fig Cross Reference. The rudder is attached to the vertical fin with three AN4-10A bolts, three AN960-416 washers (one under the bolt head and two under the nut) and a AN365-428A nut. The rudder hinges have NMB p/n MS14104-4 bearings installed and staked on each side of the 1/4"-thick hinge fitting. Since there is a certain amount of drag with these spherical bearings, it is very important to fully torque (100 inch-pounds at the nut) the three AN4-10A bolts that join the rudder to the fin. If the bolts are left too loose, they will turn on the fin hinge brackets and cause the holes to elongate.

The bearings experience little wear, but if it is necessary to replace them, construct a puller which will apply opposing forces to the outer ring of the bearing and against the aluminum housing. An AN4 bolt through both parts of the puller may be tightened until the bearing rim is pulled past the stake marks and the bearing comes out of the housing. Re-stake with a center punch after a new bearing is installed.

The rudder horn assembly is p/n 70092-4, and includes two p/n 70093-1 bushings installed for wear where the cable attaches. The horn is connected to the rudder assembly with two AN4C-7A bolts and provides rudder control stops on either side of the horn.

Control limits are shown in the Type Certificate Data sheet and below for convenience:

  • Left: 20° ± 1.0°
  • Right: 19° ± 1.0°

It is recommended that the p/n 71164-1 cable guard be and remain installed on all aircraft. Aircraft damage due to a loose rudder cable may be caused by the loss of the cable attaching hardware--erosion of the cotter pin from fertilizer blasting may allow a complete loss of the attaching bolt and nut. Air Tractor has developed this stainless steel guard to the rudder cable attach hardware to prevent such an occurrence.
