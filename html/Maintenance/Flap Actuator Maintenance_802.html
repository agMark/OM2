This section provides for guidance on maintaining the C100168-4 flap actuator and gives instructions on recommended inspections.

Actuator Issues, Wear, and Failures

To reduce the potential for confusion concerning the name of parts used in the flap actuator, refer to the following figures.

The following actuator issues have been observed. These issues should be particularly watched for while maintaining and using the flap actuator.

Issue Discussion
Motor coupling wear and failures Refer to . This is the most commonly reported actuator issue. The coupling's life is influenced by a number of factors. The coupling should be inspected regularly and replaced when wear and slack is noticed.
Worm and drive gear wear Refer to . The worm and gear teeth are constantly meshing and causing microscopic wear. This is why a special lubricant is used inside the gearbox. The worm and gear should be inspected for looseness and metal chips and should be kept clean and lubricated.
Worm gear shaft bushing wear Refer to . This issue was published in SIL 802-0021A and SIL 602-0004. Bushing wear caused the worm shaft to no longer engage the gear properly. The bushing should be inspected for wear and looseness.
Jackshaft / Ball Spline wear and looseness The jackshaft and ball spline mechanism will naturally wear with age. General condition of the shaft should be periodically observed and the actuator should be inspected for wear and looseness.
Jackshaft retention nut failure Refer to . The nut shown in is staked against the jackshaft to prevent rotation. There have been reports of insufficient staking (SL338) allowing for the nut to back off which results in the jackshaft separating from the actuator. The nut should be verified to be positioned as shown and the staking marks should be verified to be intact.
End stop failure Refer to . This failure ultimately resulted in the welded end stop that the actuator now features. It is highly unlikely that the welded end stop will fail but it should be periodically checked for security.
Various motor issues such as weakness, low motion requiring replacement of the motor Motor performance will naturally degrade for almost all electromechanical devices. The flap actuator is no different. Sound and speed changes indicate that the motor is nearing the end of its useful life.
Motor / gearbox attachment failure Refer to . There were reports of motor studs failing in service (SL375.) While this is an isolated issue, the studs and nuts should be checked periodically for security.

Inspection Procedures

Procedure 1 — 100 Hour Motor Coupling Inspection

  1. Rotate the flap coupling by hand to inspect the flap coupling for distortion or splits in the rubber. If any distortion or splits are found, replace the coupling.
  2. While rotating the coupling, feel for any play or backlash. If any play or backlash is felt, remove the coupling and perform Procedure 3 detailed below.

Procedure 2 — 100 Hour Actuator Free Play Inspection

  1. Remove left side fuselage skin below the cockpit door to gain access to the flap actuator mounting location.
  2. With the flaps retracted, firmly apply upwards and downwards loads (approximately 20 lbs.) to the trailing edge of the flap. Observe for free play between the ball spline nut and the gearbox. A maximum of .015 inches is permissible. Observe for any relative motion between the jackshaft and gearbox. Measurable motion is not acceptable. During these tests, some flexing of the flap system and connection points is normal.
  3. Repeat step 2 with flaps set to half down.
  4. Repeat step 2 with flaps set to fully down.

Procedure 3 — 300 Hour Motor Coupling Inspection

  1. Remove the flap motor from the flap actuator to access the coupling.
  2. Inspect the rubber for splits or distortion when viewed from the front or side. The angle between the metal bars should show no more than 5° deviation from the standard 90°, with no bends in the individual bars. The existence of any of these conditions requires replacement of the coupling. See for reference.

The following inspection procedure can be done to extend the flap actuator overhaul time interval from 400 hours to 1500 hours. Flap actuators that do not have this inspection procedure performed must be overhauled at the standard 400 hour interval.

Procedure 4 — 300 Hour Actuator Inspection for Overhaul Interval Extension

  1. Remove the flap actuator from aircraft.
  2. Clean all grease, grime, and debris from the actuator.
  3. Inspect bushing installed in gearbox cover (airframe attachment point) for looseness. A maximum of 0.005 inches of clearance is acceptable with the attachment bolt. Replace the bushings if required.
  4. Inspect the tee arms and threads for wear, damage, and cracking.
  5. Remove the motor and inspect the motor coupling per Procedure 3 above. Inspect the motor shaft and worm shaft end that engages the motor coupling for damage or distortion. If the worm shaft is damaged, replacement or overhaul of the flap actuator is required.
  6. Inspect the motor studs for damage or cracks. Replace the motor if the studs are damaged.
  7. Inspect the jackshaft and ball spline nut by checking for free play at 3 locations along the actuator's travel (retracted + .25 in., middle, and extended - .25 in.). The maximum allowable free play is 0.015 inches.
  8. Inspect the end stop for damage and deformation.
  9. Remove the gearbox cover; retain the o-ring. It does not need to be replaced unless it is nicked or damaged.
  10. Inspect inside the gearbox for metal debris and the presence of grease. The presence of metal chips and filings requires the replacement or overhaul of the flap actuator.
  11. Manually turn the worm shaft and verify proper engagement and function of the worm and gear.
  12. Remove the bearing cover and inspect the worm shaft bearing. The bearing must rotate freely and be free of any damage. Any bearing damage requires replacement or overhaul of the flap actuator.
  13. Inspect and clean the bearing installed in the gearbox cover. The bearing must rotate freely and be free of any damage. Any bearing damage requires replacement or overhaul of the flap actuator.
  14. Inspect the worm shaft bushing for wear. The maximum allowable shaft free play is 0.005 inches. Excessive fre play requires replacement and/or overhaul of the the flap actuator.
  15. Use parts/brake cleaner to clean the gearbox and internal components. Allow the gearbox to completely dry to make sure there is no cleaning chemical present before the gearbox is relubricated.
  16. Inspect the retention nut and staking imprints. If the nut is loose or the staking imprints are damaged, the flap actuator requires replacement or overhaul.
  17. Lubricate the gearbox and bearings with 9 - 10 mL of B100-24 (APP) lubricant. No other lubricants are authorized.
  18. Reinstall the bearing and gearbox covers.
  19. Install the motor coupling and motor.
  20. Reinstall the flap actuator.

Air Tractor suggests that you procure the necessary supplies prior to performing the inspection outlined in Procedure 4. Contact your dealer or Air Tractor Customer Service to procure supplies.

Item Description
B100-24 Lubricant. You will use approximately 10 mL every time you perform this inspection procedure.
MS29561-128 O-Ring. Needs to be replaced if damaged or worn. This should not need to be done every time you perform this inspection procedure.
