Details of the elevator controls are shown below. The aft elevator pushrod connects to the elevator horns and is adjustable on the forward end (which has a Fafnir bearing installed). The aft end of the pushrod has an NMB bearing pressed and staked into place identically to those used in the elevators and rudder. If it is to be removed, see Section 2.45.12.

The forward end of the aft pushrod is attached to the aft elevator idler. This idler has two roller bearings and a bushing installed to mount it to the fuselage. Washers on each side of the idler contain the bearings. The idler should be removed during the 12-month inspections and the bearings cleaned and packed with general purpose grease. Check the idler for excessive wobble on the lower end of the idler which could indicate worn bearings, a worn bushing, or both. Grease the bolt before reinstalling and torque to 100 in-lb at the nut. After installation, check the aft pushrod to ensure that it will rotate slightly in either direction to indicate that the rod end bearing is positioned properly.

Also remove the control stick lock and push the elevator through the full range of travel while checking the clearance of the bolt through the elevator control horns with the fuselage structure. Since two washers are installed on this bolt, one may be under the bolt head, or both may be under the nut, depending on which arrangement gives the best clearance to the fuselage structure.

Check the position of the eyebolt in the aft idler that the tail wheel lock spring is attached to (if equipped). If the eyebolt is positioned correctly, the spring end will slide easily through the eyebolt as the elevator is moved from down to full up. A little grease at this point will help.

The large aluminum pushrod connects the aft elevator idler to the forward idler. The pushrod has a rod end bearing installed with rollpins at each end. If the bearings are worn they can be replaced, but be sure to order them drilled for assembly. The aluminum pushrod is attached to the lower end of the aft idler with an eyebolt that is attached to the elevator downspring. The downspring is attached to the fuselage through a strap and provides downward stick force. The forward end of the pushrod is attached to the upper end of the forward elevator idler. If the airplane is parked outside in a moderate wind with the side skins removed, the aluminum pushrod will vibrate excessively, so it is a good idea to only have one side skin removed at a time if working outside in the wind.

The forward idler is attached to the fuselage frame through two bearings and a spacer bushing. the bushing allows the bolt to be fully torqued to 100 in-lb. at the nut. The forward idler will have end play when installed. Up to 0.040 inches of end play is acceptable.

On AT-502A/502B airplanes a pushrod connects the forward elevator idler to the control stick through the aft cockpit wall.

On AT-504 airplanes, a pushrod connects the forward elevator idler to the elevator interconnect torque tube. The left and right control stick are then connected to the torque tube with pushrods. These pushrods have an adjustable rod end bearing on the forward end.

The elevator max travel stops are located with the elevator control horns in the aft fuselage. The elevator down stop is located forward of the elevator control and should be adjusted so that the down elevator travel is 16°±1°. the elevator max up travel stop is located on a bracket aft of the elevator control horns and should be adjusted so that the up elevator travel is 28°±1° for the AT-502A and 29°±1° for the AT-502B. Check the rigging by moving the control sick through the full range of motion. The control stick should be able to move forward and aft at all aileron deflections without making contact with anythign in the cockpit.

WARNING

There have been loss of control accidents that were most likely caused by hardware in the elevator control system being loose or missing. Be sure to check and re-check that the hardware is installed and torqued to the proper values.
