The tail wheel fork housing is attached to the tail spring with two AN12H-15A bolts, secured together with MS20995C32 safety wire. These bolts have a tendency to loosen in service, especially during the first 100 hours of operation, as paint between the mating surfaces is compressed. At 100-hour intervals, the tailwheel should be jacked clear of the ground (with the locking pin in place) and given a vigorous side-to-side tug to check for movement between the housing and tail spring. See for recommended replacement times and bolt torques. Be sure to grease the bolts with MIL-A-907E antiseize lubricant or equivalent before installation. An added precaution is to apply this lubricant on the threads of the housing to prevent the bolts from seizing.

A brass wear plate is attached at the lower plate of the fork housing assembly. This plate (p/n 40018-2) is attached with countersunk machine screws and should be replaced if worn to the point where the machine screw heads are flush. The large bronze bushings inside the housing (p/n 40023-1 (lower) and 40024-1 (upper)) are pressed into place and may be driven out with a pin punch. The lower bushing has a chamfered end positioned on the lower extreme of the housing at the brass wear plate. The housing assembly is p/n 40089-1 (which is less bushings, wear plate, attaching hardware and grease fitting).
