This section provides for guidance on maintaining the flap actuator and gives instructions on recommended inspections.

Actuator Issues, Wear, and Failures

To reduce the potential for confusion concerning the name of parts used in the flap actuator, refer to the following figures.

The following actuator issues have been observed. These issues should be particularly watched for while maintaining and using the flap actuator.

Issue Discussion
Motor coupling wear and failures Refer to . This is the most commonly reported actuator issue. The coupling's life is influenced by a number of factors. The coupling should be inspected regularly and replaced when wear and slack is noticed.
Worm and drive gear wear Refer to . The worm and gear teeth are constantly meshing and causing microscopic wear. This is why a special lubricant is used inside the gearbox. The worm and gear should be inspected for looseness and metal chips and should be kept clean and lubricated.
Worm gear shaft bushing wear Refer to . This issue was published in SIL 802-0021A and SIL 602-0004. Bushing wear caused the worm shaft to no longer engage the gear properly. The bushing should be inspected for wear and looseness.
Jackshaft / Ball Spline wear and looseness The jackshaft and ball spline mechanism will naturally wear with age. Genearl condition of the shaft should be periodically observed and the actuator should be inspected for wear and looseness.
Jackshaft retention nut failure Refer to . The nut shown in is staked against the jackshaft to prevent rotation. There have been reports of insufficient staking (SL338) allowing for the nut to back off which results in the jackshaft separating from the actuator. The nut should be verified to be positioned as shown and the staking marks should be verified to be intact.
End stop failure Refer to . This failure ultimately resulted in the welded end stop that the actuator now features. It is highly unlikely that the welded end stop will fail but it should be periodically checked for security.
Various motor issues such as weakness, low motion requiring replacement of the motor Motor performance will naturally degrade for almost all electromechanical devices. The flap actuator is no different. Sound and speed changes indicate that the motor is nearing the end of its useful life.
Motor / gearbox attachment failure Refer to . There were reports of motor studs failing in service (SL375.) While this is an isolated issue, the studs and nuts should be checked periodically for security.

Inspection Procedures

Procedure 1 - 100 Hour Motor Coupling Inspection

  1. Rotate the flap coupling by hand to inspect the flap coupling for distortion or splits in the rubber. If any distortion or splits are found, replace the coupling.
  2. While rotating the coupling, feel for any play or backlash. If any play or backlash is felt, remove the coupling and perform Procedure 3 detailed below.

Procedure 2 - 100 Hour Actuator Free Play Inspection

  1. Remove left side fuselage skin below the cockpit door to gain access to the flap actuator mounting location.
  2. With the flaps retracted, firmly apply upwards and downwards loads (approximately 20 lbs.) to the trailing edge of the flap. Observe for free play between the ball spline nut and the gearbox. A maximum of .015 inches is permissible. Observe for any relative motion between the jackshaft and gearbox. Measurable motion is not acceptable. During these tests, some flexing of the flap system and connection points is normal.
  3. Repeat step 2 with flaps set to half down.
  4. Repeat step 2 with flaps set to fully down.

Procedure 3 - 300 Hour Motor Coupling Inspection

  1. Remove the flap motor from the flap actuator to access the coupling.
  2. Inspect the rubber for splits or distortion when viewed from the front or side. The angle between the metal bars should show no more than 5° deviation from the standard 90°, with no bends in the individual bars. The existence of any of these conditions requires replacement of the coupling. See for reference.
