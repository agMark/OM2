Details of the main-gear spring are shown below in Fig Cross Reference. The main gear spring (p/n 40086-1) will fit on either side of the aircraft. The spring is machined of E-4340 aircraft quality steel and formed, heat treated and shot peened for fatigue resistance. It is finally sandblasted, primed with an epoxy primer (US Paint R9006 Epoxy Primer) and finished with Alumigrip G-9046 yellow polyurethane paint.

Each gear spring is clamped to the fuselage frame with a p/n 40080-1 clamp block, one NAS154A-114 bolt and one NAS152DH-43 bolt. MS20002C12 washers go under the aft bolt head and MS20002C14 washers go under the forward bolt head (making sure the chamfered side of the washer is directly against the bolt head in both cases). Various quantities of round 3088A324 shim washers go between the clamp block and fuselage frame aft bolt while 3088A325 shims are used between the clamp block and fuselage frame forward bolt. The 3088A*** shims are .010 thick and are selected to allow the bolts to be fully torqued without bending the clamp block. However, if too many thick shims are installed, the gear connection will be too loose and will rattle during taxi or landing. If the gear leg becomes loose during service (even though the clamp block bolts are fully torqued) it will be necessary to re-shim the clamp block.

The inboard attachment of the spring to the fuselage frame uses a NAS156A-88 bolt, MS20002C16 washer under the bolt head (with the chamfered side of the washer next to the head), one or more MS20002-16 washers under the nut, AN310-16 nut and a MS24665-360 cotter pin. The bolt is installed head down and 6,400 inch-pounds of torque is applied to the bolt head. If you are unable to install the cotter pin after torquing, tighten the nut until the next castellation aligns (but never to exceed 60° of additional rotation).

It is necessary to hoist the aircraft as described under when changing the inboard bolt. Be sure to grease the bolt with general purpose grease before installing. Also verify the bolt is marked "R" on the head, indicating the threads were rolled after heat-treatment.

The main spring installation method employed at the factory determines the number of shims required for installation of the clamp block by fully torquing the bolts before attempting to install the large inboard bolt. The block is checked for straightness with a small straight edge (a 6" steel scale is fine) to ensure the block is not bent due to insufficient shims. The gear leg is then grasped at the lower end and given an tug towards the wingtip. A moderate tug should move the inboard end of the tapered leg down from the attach bushing in the fuselage frame a distance of 1/8" to 1/4". If a distance greater than 1/4" is observed, the connection is too loose and a thinner shim must tried and the process repeated. Once the proper combination of shims is determined, the bolts are loosened and the large inboard bolt installed. The clamp block bolts are then fully torqued--3,000 inch-pounds on the aft bolt and 4,000 inch-pounds (head) or 3,500 inch-pounds (nut) on the forward bolt.

Since main gear springs and clamp blocks may vary in thickness due to machining tolerances, it is necessary to make shim selections as described above if either the gear spring or clamp block are changed.

When ordering new main gear springs or clamp blocks, consider ordering additional 3088A324 and 3088A325 shims in case they are needed. In addition, the large aluminum tapered shims at the axle attachment might need an addition or change so it would be a good idea to order a variety of p/n 40083-1/-2/-3 tapered shims as well. Whenever the airplane is ground-looped or involved in an incident which puts a permanent bend in one gear spring, the other spring may have been overstressed and must be replaced.
