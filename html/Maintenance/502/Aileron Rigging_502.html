1. Verify that the flaps are correctly rigged.
  2. Set the control lock and retract the flaps.
  3. Check that the distance of the pushrod located between the flap torque tube and the 90° flap interconnect bellcrank measures 6.65” from bearing hole to bearing hole.
  4. Check that the length of the pushrod between the 90° flap interconnect bellcrank and the rectangular welded support frame assembly at the wing root measures 13.78” from bearing hole to bearing hole.
  5. Check that the length of the pushrod between the control stick torque tube horn and the 180° bellcrank measures 19.88” from bearing hole to bearing hole.
  6. Ensure that the bottom of the support frame assembly clears the vertical tube of the fuselage by 1/4”.
  7. Adjust the pushrod between the wing mounted bellcrank and the aileron control horn so that the trailing edge of the aileron is 1/8” to 3/16” below the trailing edge of the flap (this allows flight loads to bring the trailing edge of the aileron up, approximately even to the flap trailing edge).
  8. Check the aileron travel (with flaps up). The aileron travel is 20° up (+/- 1°) and 14° down (+/- 1°). The aileron control stops are located under the cockpit floor on each side of the control stick torque tube horn. The adjustment consists of tightening or loosening the bolts that compress a stack of 6 or 7 neoprene washers. The neoprene washers act as shock absorbers for the control system, but sometimes split and fall out, so it is advisable to inspect the stops and replace the neoprene washers as necessary during 12-month inspections.
  9. Inspect the aileron control system for interference, rubbing, or binding. Inspect the flap/aileron control system clearance per section XXX.XXX.

There is an aileron stop bracket attached to the wing rib near the wing mounted bellcrank. It is designed so that the aileron bellcrank contacts a spring loaded plunger, and then as the aileron moves farther, a hard stop establishes the final limit of aileron travel. The hard stop can be adjusted by loosening the jam nut, then twisting the bolt to adjust the stop. The stop should be adjusted to allow 20° (+/- 1°) of up aileron travel.

There has been one incident recorded of a parked aircraft experiencing a hard wind storm from the rear, causing deflection of the stop bracket sufficiently so that the plunger slipped past the bellcrank arm and would not return. If this occurs, the ailerons are frozen in the fully deflected position.

To prevent this from happening in flight, Air Tractor redesigned the bracket that holds the spring plunger to be more rigid and to eliminate the possibility that the plunger will slip past the arm. Operators should verify that the bellcrank is striking the plunger solidly.
