The Pratt & Whitney PT6A series of engines have been proven to be one of the most reliable power plants available today. The engine is a lightweight, reverse flow, free-turbine engine utilizing two independent sections--the gas generator section and the power (or hot) section.

Air enters the engine through an annular plenum chamber, passing through a three-stage axial and single-stage centrifugal compressor. In the combustion chamber, fuel mixed with air is ignited. The expanding gasses are directed through the compressor turbine, past the power turbine and out the exhaust ports on opposing sides of the engine. In turn, the power turbine rotates the propeller shaft via a reduction gearbox (RGB).

The standard engine for the AT-402B is the Pratt & Whitney PT6A-15AG turboprop engine. This engine is rated at 680 SHP for takeoff at sea level and 71 °F. Maximum takeoff torque is 1628 ft-lbs., maximum ITT is 725 °C, maximum Ng is 101.5% and maximum Np for all operations is 2200 RPM.

The standard engine for the AT-402A is the PT6A-11AG, BS 943. This engine uses a gas generator similar to the PT6A-15 AG with a propeller reduction gearbox rated at 550 SHP. Maximum takeoff torque is 1315 ft-lbs. with a maximum ITT of 700 °C.

For long engine life it is recommended that the ITT be kept well below the maximum limits. Use only enough power on takeoff to operate safely from the strip being used.
